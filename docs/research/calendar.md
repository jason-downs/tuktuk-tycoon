Compiled 2026-09-26 for *Tuk-Tuk Tycoon: Chiang Mai*. Independently re-checked the same day against the primary files: the MOTS spreadsheets (every monthly Chiang Mai row), the BOT holiday notice, the WMO climate table, the CMU 2026 calendar PDF, TomTom's embedded 2025 data, Open-Meteo CAMS/ERA5 and OSM geometry. - Records (1951–present): 3.7 °C (Jan) to 42.5 °C (Apr and May; the text dates the all-time high to May 2016). [cm-wiki]
- **Time of day for rain (May–Oct):** in ERA5 reanalysis for 2016–2025 (my own computation from [era5]), rain peaks in the afternoon and evening. The share of seasonal rainfall per hour is 5.0–6.5% for each hour from 12:00 to 18:00, against 2.5–3.0% for each hour from 06:00 to 10:00. The chance that a given hour has ≥0.5 mm of rain is 21–25% from 13:00 to 17:00 and 11–13% from 08:00 to 11:00. ERA5 roughly doubles the station monthly totals (Aug: 412 mm ERA5 mean vs 223 mm station), so use it for the hourly *shape* only. Night rain is common too: 16–20% of hours from 20:00 to 23:00 are wet. [era5]
- **Ping River floods (late Sep–early Oct):** the flood threshold is +3.7 m at gauge P.1 (Nawarat Bridge). Major floods listed: 2024-10-05 at +5.30 m (656 m³/s), with the city flooded for 102 h; 2022-10-05 at +4.75 m (630 m³/s); early Oct 2011 at +4.95 m (700 m³/s); 2005 at +4.90 m then +4.93 m (82 h). [cmdoctor-flood] 2024 had **two** floods about 10 days apart: late Sep at +4.93 m, then early Oct (5.25 m at 05:00 on the day Thai PBS reported, the highest in 50 years). Chang Klan Road and the Night Bazaar were under "about a metre" of water with strong currents, Arcade (Arkhet) bus terminal was under deep water, and some rail sections were closed. [tpbs-flood]

## 3. Smoky / burning season (Feb–Apr, sometimes Jan–May)

- Thailand's PM2.5 standard (June 2023) is a 24-hour mean of 37.5 µg/m³ and an annual mean of 15 µg/m³. For 2013–2022, the haze-season (Dec–May) mean PM2.5 was 44.0 µg/m³ in Chiang Mai (42.9 ± 25.7 across the upper north, vs 12.1 in non-haze months), with the peak in March. [pmc-decadal] Earlier ground-station maxima include 155 µg/m³ hourly at station 35T (City Hall) on 30 Mar 2018. [search summary of a conference paper (unverified)] Wikipedia says March is "the worst month to visit Chiang Mai", and PM2.5 reached 183 µg/m³ in 2018. [cm-wiki]
- **2026 smog event:** it began in Jan 2026. PM2.5 exceeded 300 µg/m³ in some districts on 1 Apr, and Chiang Mai had AQI 231 (world #2) on 2 Apr. On 4 Apr, Chiang Mai, Lamphun and Phayao were declared emergency disaster zones. Hospital patient numbers doubled. [smog26-wiki]
- **Modelled monthly PM2.5** (CAMS via Open-Meteo, city centre; my computation from [cams]; this model reads lower than ground stations, so use the shape, not the absolute level):
  2025: Jan 31 · Feb 31 · **Mar 44** · Apr 30 · May 17 · Jun–Oct 14–19 · Nov 19 · Dec 27 µg/m³.
  2026: Jan 28 · Feb 30 · Mar 38 · **Apr 54** (27 days over 37.5 µg/m³) · May 16 · Jun–Sep 10–13.
- **Effect on tourism:**
  - In 2026, March visitors fell 4.5% YoY and occupancy was 65.4%, against 89.5% in February. [mots26]
  - Hotel bookings for Songkran 2026 were down 50%. Occupancy in early April 2026 was "barely 50 percent", against the usual 80–90%. Airfares were also up 20–30% because crude was above $100. [crt-songkran26]
  - More than 1 bn THB in tourism revenue was lost over roughly 60 days in 2026, and occupancy fell to 30–40% before Songkran. [search summary citing chiangraitimes (unverified)]
  - Blogs advise leaving the north for Feb–Apr, and some trails and national parks close. [sea-backpacker]
  - TomTom's monthly congestion bottoms out in March (37% in 2025, 39% in 2024) [tomtom], which is consistent with fewer tourists plus school holidays.

## 4. Festivals and events: dates, places, closures

### New Year
A cabinet resolution of 12 Nov 2024 made Fri 2 Jan 2026 a special holiday, giving a 5-day break from 31 Dec 2025 to 4 Jan 2026. [bot-2026] [thaiger-hol]

### Bo Sang Umbrella & San Kamphaeng Handicraft Festival
- **When:** Fri–Sun 16–18 Jan 2026 (41st edition). [tat-bosang] That is the 3rd weekend of January; treating it as a fixed rule is my inference (unverified).
- **Where:** Bo Sang village (Ban Bosang, 18.7675 N, 99.0836 E, OSM node 248907803), about **10 km** east of Tha Phae Gate in a straight line, off the San Kamphaeng road. [osm-poi] Road distance is longer (unverified).
- **What:** a parade of "Bo Sang beauties" riding bicycles with parasols ("over 500 dancers"), a Kad Mua market with 30+ stalls, and umbrella-painting. [tat-bosang]

### Chiang Mai Flower Festival (49th)
- **When:** Fri–Sun 13–15 Feb 2026, open 09:00–21:00. [thansett-flower] [prd-flower] Wikipedia says the "first weekend of February", but the 2026 edition was on the 2nd weekend. [wiki-flower]
- **Where:** Nong Buak Haad Park (inside the Old City's SW corner, 18.7823 N, 98.9793 E [osm-poi]) and the plaza at Tha Phae Gate. About 170,000 plants of 52 varieties were on show in 2026. [thansett-flower]
- **Parade:** Sat 14 Feb 2026. Floats set off from 07:30 and the opening ceremony/parade is from 08:00, starting at the foot of Nawarat Bridge ("เริ่มจากบริเวณสะพานนวรัฐ มุ่งหน้าสู่สวนสาธารณะหนองบวกหาด"). [prd-flower] [thansett-flower] The exact form-up spot "in front of the Governor's residence" is not in the official release (unverified). There were about 23 floats and 9 bands. [thansett-flower]
- **Why the 2nd weekend in 2026:** the 1st weekend (7–8 Feb) was the general-election weekend with an alcohol ban. [thairanked-dry] That this caused the move is my inference (unverified).
- **Route:** Nawarat Bridge → Tha Phae Rd → Kotchasarn Rd → Chang Lo Rd → Arak Rd → Nong Buak Haad. [pmtours-flower] The official releases give only the start and end points. Wikipedia independently describes the same shape: floats line up on **Charoen Muang Rd** (east bank, Nawarat Bridge towards the railway station), go along Tha Phae Rd to Tha Phae Gate, "turn left" and follow the moat to Suan Buak Haad; it notes the parade is slow with frequent stops. Its "16:00" start time is out of date: the 2026 parade started at 08:00. [wiki-flower] Arrival time at the park is unverified.

### Chinese New Year
- **When:** Tue 17 Feb 2026 (Year of the Horse). The tour operator describes a **two-day** celebration but gives no exact 2026 dates for it (unverified). [pmtours-cny]
- **Where:** Warorot Market (Kad Luang, 18.7902 N, 99.0005 E [osm-poi]), the Guan Yu shrine, and the Chinese gate on Chang Moi Rd.
- **Parade:** starts 08:30 at Tha Phae Gate and reaches the Guan Yu shrine at about 10:00.
- **Closures:** the riverside street by Warorot is "closed off to traffic" during the celebrations, particularly at night, and the guide says to "avoid driving near the Ping River/Warorot area". [pmtours-cny] The source names no other closed streets; closures on Chang Moi or Tha Phae Rd are unverified. (Tour operator only; nothing from the municipality.)

### Makha Bucha
Tue 3 Mar 2026. This is a public holiday and a nationwide alcohol-sale ban day. [bot-2026] [thairanked-dry]

### Songkran (Thai New Year)
- **Official holiday:** Mon–Wed 13–15 Apr 2026. [bot-2026] Chakri Day (Mon 6 Apr) is also a holiday.
- **Chiang Mai programme:** 6–17 Apr 2026, with activity peaking on 10–15 Apr. [timeout-sk26]
- **Moat splash zone:** the Old City moat roads are the "official splash zone", looping through the Jaeng Hua Rin (NW) and Jaeng Si Phum (NE) corners and past Tha Phae Gate; the source gives no separate dates for the zone, so 13–15 Apr is the traditional-days assumption (unverified). People fill water guns from the moat. In 2026 the zone was designated alcohol-free. [timeout-sk26]
- **Other venues:** Tha Phae Gate and Ratchadamnoen Rd are a car-free family zone with a two-storey water tunnel on Tha Phae Rd plus performances and contests. Nimman venues: Maya (11–15 Apr) and Think Park (12–15) with concerts and water cannons; One Nimman has a kids' pool party (its dates are unverified). Hotel parties run along Chang Klan / the Night Bazaar. New out-of-town zones in 2026: Mae Kuang Dam and Oasis Saraphi. [timeout-sk26] Water fights "intensify around 10–11 Apr". [timeout-sk26]
- **Mon 13 Apr 2026 schedule:** [citylife-sk13]
  - 08:00 bicycle-and-parasol procession from Buddhasathan to Tha Phae Gate.
  - 08:30–10:00 opening at Ton Yang on the Chiang Mai–Lamphun Rd, with temporary closures.
  - **14:09** sacred Buddha images procession (Phra Buddha Sihing): Nawarat Bridge → Tha Phae Rd → Ratchadamnoen Rd → Wat Phra Singh, where people bathe the image. [citylife-sk13] [cmalacarte-sk]
  - **17:00–24:00** "World Songkran Tha Pae 2026", with Tha Phae Rd **closed from Wat Saen Fang to Tha Phae Gate**.
  - 18:00–24:00 events at Maya, One Nimman and Think Park.
  - A temporary roundabout at the Nong Hoi intersection was to be activated if traffic on the Chiang Mai–Lamphun Rd became heavy.
- **Demand:** see the 2025 figures in §1. For 2026, smog cut bookings in half. [crt-songkran26] Nationwide, TAT **projected** (not reported) 30.35 bn THB of tourism revenue for 11–15 Apr 2026 (+6%), 5.96 M domestic trips (+7%) and about 500,000 international arrivals (+4%); the release has no Chiang Mai breakdown. [tat-sk26]

### Inthakin (City Pillar) Festival
- **When:** 13–19 May 2026, closing ceremony 20 May. [citylife-inthakin] The date follows the Lanna lunar calendar and falls around May–June (general pattern; unverified).
- **Where:** Wat Chedi Luang, open 06:00–24:00 for **Sai Khan Dok** flower offerings.
- **Procession:** 13 May from 08:00, the Phra Chao Saen Ha image is carried from Wat Chang Taem to Wat Chedi Luang, followed by an afternoon procession into the Old City.
- **TAT tram tour:** 14–18 May, 18:00–20:00, 40 people a day. It runs from Three Kings Monument → Chang Moi → Kad Luang → Tha Phae Gate → Wat Chedi Luang. [citylife-inthakin]

- Visakha Bucha: Sun 31 May 2026, observed as a holiday on Mon 1 Jun. [bot-2026]
- Asarnha Bucha: Wed 29 Jul. Khao Phansa: Thu 30 Jul (a government-only holiday). [harwell] [bot-2026]
- Ok Phansa: Mon 26 Oct 2026. This is an alcohol-ban day but not a public holiday. [thairanked-dry]

### Loy Krathong / Yi Peng 2026
- **How the date is set:** Loy Krathong is the full moon of the 12th Thai lunar month, which in Lanna reckoning is *duean yi* (month 2, hence "Yi Peng"). Wikipedia describes a 3-day festival "culminating on the full moon". In practice the city programme brackets the full moon: 4–6 Nov in 2025, and 23–25 Nov in 2026 around the full moon on the 24th. Dates: 2024-11-15, 2025-11-05, **2026-11-24 (Tue)**, 2027-11-13. [wiki-loykrathong] [wiki-yipeng]
- **Municipality programme 2026:** **23–25 Nov 2026**, with the grand krathong/float parade on **25 Nov**. The municipality asks for a **strict ban on sky-lantern (khom loi) releases in the city**. [cmnews-yp26]
- **Crowd sites** (unverified for 2026, based on past years):
  - Tha Phae Gate plaza (lantern displays and contests, 18:30–22:30).
  - The Ping River bank between **Nawarat Bridge and the Iron Bridge** (krathong floating).
  - Three Kings Monument (candle lighting on the first evening).
  - Temples: Wat Phan Tao, Wat Chedi Luang, Wat Phra Singh, Wat Lok Moli.
  - Khlong Mae Kha. [chiangmaitraveller-yp25] [beautifulcm-yp]
  - One travel site claims "free public lantern releases at Tha Phae Gate" in 2026. [beautifulcm-yp] **This conflicts with the official city ban, so do not use it.**
- **Parade route:** in 2025 the grand parade ran on 6 Nov from 19:00, from Tha Phae Gate plaza along Tha Phae Rd towards the Ping River, with 31 lanterns (1 municipal, 5 sponsored, 25 competing). [nation-yp25] [chiangmaitraveller-yp25] The Nation calls the end point "Chiang Mai City Hall", but Wikipedia says it concludes "in front of the Chiang Mai City Municipality Office" [wiki-yipeng]. That office is on the west bank near Warorot at **18.7955 N, 98.9986 E** (OSM way 695914720), not the provincial City Hall (Sala Klang), which is about 5.5 km NNW at 18.8371 N, 98.9708 E. [osm-poi] The street path between Tha Phae Rd and the office is unverified. One travel site gives Tha Phae Gate → Ratchadamnoen Rd → Three Kings instead, 19:00, about 2 km. [beautifulcm-yp] (unverified; conflicts with the two sources above)
- **2025 festival nights:** also used Khlong Mae Kha and Si Khong pier, and had a light show along the Ping River. [nation-yp25] Yi Peng 2025 was reported to have put 2.7 bn THB into the local economy (Bangkok Post, 11 Nov 2025, as cited on [wiki-yipeng]; article not fetched, so unverified).
- **Closures:** "Moat road closes to most traffic 19:00–23:00 inside the Old City". [beautifulcm-yp] (unverified)
- **Lantern rules in 2025:** releases were banned outright in red zones in 6 districts: all of Mueang Chiang Mai and Hang Dong, plus 5 subdistricts of Saraphi, 1 of San Sai, 5 of Mae Rim and 1 of San Pa Tong. Elsewhere, sky lanterns were allowed only on 5–6 Nov between 19:00 and 01:00 in approved zones, with permission requested via the "Bampen" app. The penalty is up to 5 years and/or a 200,000 THB fine. [thaiger-ban25]
- **Airport:** on the 2025 lantern nights (5–6 Nov), all flight operations ended by 19:00. 161 flights were affected (65 cancelled: 41 domestic, 24 international; 96 retimed) and 44 extra flights were added afterwards. Passengers were expected to exceed 32,000/day (+17%). [nation-cnx-yp25] The airport area's centroid is 3.8 km SW of Tha Phae Gate. [osm-poi]
- **Ticketed mass releases** (outside the city): CAD Khom Loi at Doi Saket/Mae Taeng, 4,500–8,500 THB; the Mae Jo international release, 6,500–15,000 THB. [beautifulcm-yp] (prices unverified) CAD is scheduled for 24–25 Nov 2026 at Miracle Land, Mae Taeng. [search summary of faceticket/naewna (unverified)]
- **2025 context:** the festival was "quieter" because of national mourning, and large fireworks were cancelled. [chiangmaitraveller-yp25] TomTom's **worst traffic day of 2025 was Mon 3 Nov** (75% congestion), the start of festival week. [tomtom]

### Traffic by hour
TomTom 2025 city data gives the travel time for a 10 km drive. [tomtom]
- **Free flow:** ~17 min per 10 km at 04:00 (≈35 km/h).
- **Weekday mornings:** 07:00 ≈ 24.7 min (Mon–Fri); 08:00 ≈ 23 min.
- **Midday:** ≈ 25 min.
- **Evening peak:** 16:00 ≈ 28 min; **17:00 ≈ 29–31 min** (Fri worst at 31 min, ≈20 km/h).
- **Later evening:** 18:00 ≈ 26 min; 20:00 ≈ 23 min; 22:00 ≈ 20 min.
- **Weekends:** mornings are light (Sat/Sun 07:00 ≈ 18.8 min) and the Sun 17:00 peak is only ≈ 24 min. **Saturday midday is the weekend peak:** Sat 12:00 ≈ 27.8 min (71% congestion), Sat 11:00–15:00 all ≥ 26 min.
- **Averages (city centre, 2025):** 24 min 12 s per 10 km over all hours (24.8 km/h, 48.7% congestion). Morning rush (07:00) 24 min 41 s (24.3 km/h, 55.8%); evening rush (17:00) 29 min 42 s (20.2 km/h, 82.4%). Drivers lost 89 h in rush hour in 2025, 3 h 50 min less than in 2024.

### Nightlife and alcohol
- **Legal retail sale hours:** 11:00–24:00. The 14:00–17:00 ban was lifted for a 180-day trial from late 2025 (the 3 Dec 2025 start date is unverified) and abolished permanently by a Royal Gazette notice published Thu 28 May 2026, effective the next day, **29 May 2026**. Exemptions: hotels, licensed entertainment venues (which keep their own hours) and international airport terminals. [tat-alcohol26] [nation-alcohol26]
- **Closing times:** licensed entertainment venues in designated zones of Chiang Mai may stay open to **04:00**, a pilot in force since 15 Dec 2023. The pilot announcement named 4 provinces (Bangkok, Chonburi, Phuket, Chiang Mai) and left zoning to the Interior Ministry [nation-4am]; a later 5th area (Koh Samui) is unverified. Other venues close at 01:00 or 02:00. [nation-4am] [nation-anutin]
  - Which Chiang Mai zones? One source says "Nimmanhaemin and Old City entertainment strips". [go2thailand] (unverified)
  - Only about 10 of the 27 registered Chiang Mai entertainment venues were operating in Dec 2023. Nearly 1,000 bars trade as restaurants/bistros and cannot use the late licence. [nation-cm4am]
- **Nationwide 4 am / abolishing zoning:** PM and Interior Minister Anutin ordered a feasibility study at the 21 Oct 2025 cabinet meeting, targeting Jan 2026 via an Interior Ministry regulation. [nation-anutin] Implementation is unverified.

## 7. Traffic geography and the moat one-way system

- **Moat one-way system** (my analysis of OSM `oneway=yes` geometry versus the moat polygon; a traveller blog agrees that "the outer loop goes clockwise and inner loop goes counterclockwise"): [osm-moat] [tieland]
  - **Inner moat roads run anticlockwise:** Moon Muang Rd (east), Sri Phum Rd (north), Arak Rd (west), Bumrung Buri Rd (south).
  - **Outer moat roads run clockwise:** Chaiyaphum Rd (east, north of Tha Phae Gate), Kotchasarn Rd (east, south of Tha Phae Gate), Mani Nopharat Rd (north), Bun Rueang Rit Rd (west), Chang Lo Rd (south).
  - All moat roads are tagged `maxspeed=40` with 1–4 lanes. [osm-moat]
  - Re-checked 2026-09-26 against OSM geometry: every `oneway=yes` segment of Moon Muang (1,581 m), Sri Phum, Arak (1,591 m) and Bumrung Buri (1,474 m) runs anticlockwise, and every segment of Chaiyaphum (877 m), Kotchasarn (762 m), Mani Nopharat (1,647 m), Bun Rueang Rit (1,555 m) and Chang Lo (1,641 m) runs clockwise. [osm-moat]
  - The moat's centroid is 18.78716 N, 98.98645 E. The moat roads span about 18.7810–18.7960 N and 98.9775–98.9939 E. Tha Phae Gate (OSM node 1017379824) is on the east side at 18.78776 N, 98.99327 E. [osm-moat] [osm-poi]
- **One-way streets east of Tha Phae Gate** (OSM `oneway=yes`; direction measured on the segments nearest the gate): **Tha Phae Rd runs westbound** (river → gate) and is tagged one-way from about 99.0025 E to the gate, with the last ~250 m to Nawarat Bridge two-way (4–5 lanes). **Chang Moi Rd runs eastbound** (gate → Warorot) and **Loi Kroh Rd runs eastbound** (moat → Night Bazaar). Wualai Rd and Ratchadamnoen Rd are two-way. [osm-moat] [osm-poi] The Songkran procession and the flower parade both travel westbound on Tha Phae Rd, with the normal traffic flow.
  - Note that [cmambassador-drive] describes the whole moat as "anticlockwise", which is wrong for the outer ring.
- **Rincome (Rin Kham) intersection** (signals at 18.8013 N, 98.9678 E; MAYA mall at 18.8025 N, 98.9673 E [osm-poi]): where Huay Kaew Rd, Nimmanhaemin Rd and the Chiang Mai–Lampang Superhighway meet. Locals call it "the city intersection with the longest wait for a green light" (Nation, Jul 2019). [nation-rinkham] The claims that the Superhighway "abruptly ends and funnels traffic" into Nimman and that pedestrians wait about 5 min come only from a search summary of a blog (unverified).
- **Huay Kaew Rd:** the road from the Old City's NW corner to CMU and Doi Suthep. OSM maps it mostly as a divided road (one-way pairs). Afternoon rush congestion is common, and there are frequent police checkpoints on the Superhighway, Huay Kaew and the Old City exits. [osm-moat] [search summary (unverified)]
- **Chang Phueak Rd:** the primary road north from Chang Phueak Gate (a mix of 2-lane one-way and 4-lane two-way sections in OSM). OSM also maps the night food market just outside the gate as "Thalat Torung Chang Phuak" (18.7959 N, 98.9857 E). [osm-poi] Chang Phueak Bus Terminal 1 (provincial routes) is on this road. [cmgt-bus]
- **Arcade bus terminals 2 & 3:** long-distance buses, with about 60 operators. [mychiangmai-arcade] Terminal 2 is at 18.8008 N, 99.0172 E and Terminal 3 at 18.7996 N, 99.0178 E, about 2.9 km ENE of Tha Phae Gate. [osm-poi]
- **Event hotspots:**
  - Tha Phae Gate and Tha Phae Rd: Songkran, Yi Peng, Flower parade, CNY parade.
  - Ratchadamnoen Rd: Sunday market.
  - Wualai Rd: Saturday market.
  - Chang Klan Rd: Night Bazaar and floods.
  - Warorot / Chang Moi / the riverside: CNY.
  - The Ping River from Nawarat Bridge to the Iron Bridge: Loy Krathong.
  - Nong Buak Haad: Flower Festival.
  - Wat Chedi Luang: Inthakin.
