Romanisation of Thai / Kham Mueang is approximate and tone-less. Where it is my own transliteration of a Thai-script source, the Thai script is given too.

### Old City structure (for map logic)

- Gates (OSM): Tha Phae (E) 18.78776, 98.99327; Chang Phueak (N) 18.79543, 98.98659; Suan Dok (W) 18.78884, 98.97819 (osm:node/6717438786); Chiang Mai Gate (S) 18.78136, 98.98898 (osm:node/6107975995); Saen Pung (SW) 18.78154, 98.9816 (osm:node/11226724529).
- Corner bastions ("chaeng", OSM): Chaeng Si Phum (NE) 18.795, 98.99342 (osm:way/263459882); Chaeng Hua Lin (NW) 18.79537, 98.97994 (osm:way/317516852); Chaeng Ku Hueang (SW) 18.78184, 98.97812 (osm:way/317516851); Chaeng Katam (SE, แจ่งก๊ะต๊ำ) 18.78142, 98.99265 (osm:way/791602197).
- "Tha Phae" = "raft landing / raft pier" — goods came off Ping River rafts here ([search summary](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Chiang_Mai)); gate first built 1296, present structure rebuilt 1985–87 ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tha_Phae_Gate)).
- **Moat traffic (derived from OSM `oneway` way directions, 2026-09-26):** inner moat roads run **anticlockwise** (Sri Phum westbound on the north, Arak southbound on the west, Bumrung Buri eastbound on the south, Moon Muang northbound on the east); outer roads run **clockwise** (Mani Noppharat eastbound, Chaiyaphum/Kotchasan southbound, Bun Rueang Rit northbound). Re-derived from `data-raw/roads.json` in the fact-check pass: all one-way segments of these eight roads agree except a few short corner connectors; all carry maxspeed 40.

### Etiquette the game should respect

- **Women and monks:** a woman must not sit or stand next to a monk or brush his robes; to pass him something she puts it down for him to pick up. On buses a monk "always get[s] a seat… usually right at the back" ([Rough Guides](https://www.roughguides.com/thailand/customs-etiquette/)). On songthaews a male passenger may swap seats so a woman is not beside a monk (search summary, [backpackthailand](https://backpackthailand.com/guides/songthaew-tuk-tuk-guide); page returned 429) (unverified anecdote).
- At alms, women place offerings on a cloth/tray the monk provides (unverified; cited page now 404). This follows from the Rough Guides rule above.
- Head is sacred, feet low: don't touch heads, don't point feet at people or Buddha images ([Rough Guides](https://www.roughguides.com/thailand/customs-etiquette/)). "No feet on seats" is a common extension, not in that source.
- Temples may bar women from specific buildings: e.g. Wat Sri Suphan's ordination hall ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat_Sri_Suphan)).
- Wai: hands at chest, fingertips below the nose; return a wai, don't initiate as a foreigner (same).
- Temples: long trousers or knee-length skirts, covered arms, shoes off (same).
- Royal family: criticism is a criminal offence (same). Keep royal content out of dialogue.
- **Tipping:** "tipping drivers isn't common"; rounding up the fare or adding a small amount is courteous, especially if the driver helped or beat heavy traffic ([cleverthai](https://www.cleverthai.com/guide-when-tipping-in-thailand/)). Specific amounts (10–20 THB; 20–50 THB for luggage) (unverified; not on the cleverthai page).
- **Kinship address:** *phi* (older sibling) is the safe default for a tuk-tuk driver; *lung* (uncle) for a middle-aged man, *pa* (aunt) for a middle-aged woman vendor, *yai* for over-60s, *nong* for someone younger ([search summary, thaiworldview / thailandknowledge](https://thaiworldview.com/family.htm)).
- Cultural values: *mai pen rai* ("never mind / no problem", smooths conflict), *jai yen* ("cool heart", stay calm), *sanuk* (fun as part of daily life) ([thaizer](https://www.thaizer.com/mai-pen-rai-means-never-mind/); [wiki Sanuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanuk_(Thai_culture)); [b-side travels](https://bsidetravels.com/2019/01/17/thai-values-jai-yen-yen/)).

## 6. Names for procedurally hired drivers

- Every Thai has a given name plus a nickname (*chue len*); 80% of nicknames are one syllable ([learnthaiwithmod](https://learnthaiwithmod.com/2013/06/top-10-common-thai-nicknames-and-some-weird-ones/)).
- Nicknames are given in childhood, often humorous; unflattering ones (*Mah* dog, *Moo* pig, *Gop* frog) were meant to confuse bad spirits; others: *Nok* bird, *Daeng* red, *Uan* fat, *Thong* gold; modern: Porsche, Benz, Google ([wiki Thai name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_name)). *Lek*/*Noi* = small, *Gai* = chicken, *Yai* = big ([search summary, learnthaiwithmod / ling-app](https://ling-app.com/blog/thai-nicknames/)).
- Top 10 (Mod, 2013): May, Ploy, Nan, Ice, Bank, Nahm, Fah, New, Ball, Beer. Odd ones: Status, Airbus, Raccoon, Beaver, Chipmunk ([learnthaiwithmod](https://learnthaiwithmod.com/2013/06/top-10-common-thai-nicknames-and-some-weird-ones/)).
- Surnames are unique per family (new ones ≤10 Thai letters; 81% unique in a 45,665 sample) ([wiki Thai name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_name)) — so any invented surname is plausible, and the list in §11 is synthesised.
- Women drive tuk-tuks in Chiang Mai, though no count was found: Ya-Ya, "Thailand's first female mountain tuk-tuk driver", chemical-engineering graduate, lead driver at The Tuk Tuk Club for 5 years ([Tuk Tuk Club, Nov 2022](https://www.thetuktukclub.com/2022/11/10/thailands-first-female-mountain-tuk-tuk-driver/)). A board member of the Chiang Mai motor-samlor cooperative (สหกรณ์สามล้อเครื่องเชียงใหม่) quoted in 2022 was a woman, "Nong Oy" Rungnapa (รุ่งนภา เอกกลิ่นผล) ([chiangmainews, 12 Jul 2022](https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/news/chiangmai/2211834/)). ## 7. Chiang Mai tuk-tuks

### Regulation & fleet

- **27 Apr 2017:** a Transport Ministry announcement opened up to **450 new registrations of *electric-motor* three-wheel taxis** in Chiang Mai (the MGR headline frames it as capping tuk-tuks at 450). Applicants had to live in the province and belong to one of three bodies: Nakhon Chiang Mai Motor-Samlor Cooperative, Lanna Motor-Samlor Cooperative, Northern Tuk-Tuk Drivers Club (Chiang Mai). Rules: 1 person = 1 vehicle; no sale or transfer for 2 years; max vehicle age 10 years; lit roof "TAXI" sign in a frame ≥30 × 7.5 cm with letters ≥5 cm tall; registration number painted on both outer sides in a contrasting colour. Vehicle spec: motor ≥4 kW rated, top speed ≥45 km/h sustained for ≥30 min at gross weight; width ≤1.5 m, length ≤4 m, height ≤2 m, interior height ≥1.2 m; passengers board only on the driver's left. At the time **1,123 tuk-tuks** were registered in Chiang Mai in total ([MGR Online, 27 Apr 2017](https://mgronline.com/politics/detail/9600000042541); article body quoted in the fact-check pass).
- A cooperative had **>1,000** vehicles before COVID and **~100** by Jul 2022 ([chiangmainews](https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/news/chiangmai/2211834/)).
- Pedal samlor: only **28** left in Chiang Mai, every operator elderly, aged 60–85, no younger successors; 30–50 THB per local cargo trip, 200–300 THB for a Kad Luang → temple tourist ride ([Thai PBS, 19 Feb 2023](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/324731)).
- **Plates:** tuk-tuk taxis carry **yellow plates with green text**, series สก / สข / ศข ([wiki plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Thailand)); "สก" plates are visible in the Commons photos below.
- **Electric:** GrabTukTuk Electric launched in Chiang Mai on **4 Jun 2019**, billed "first in ASEAN"; from 12–15 THB/km; target 450 e-tuk-tuks in the network; LPG tuk-tuk fuel averages ~6,000 THB/month, EV cuts fuel cost up to 80% ([The Standard](https://thestandard.co/grab-tuk-tuk/)); partner cooperative "Nakorn Lanna" (unverified: [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/life/travel/1694284/grabtuktuk-opens-in-chiang-mai) paywalled; The Standard names no cooperative). The Grab EV tuk-tuk (promoted with PEA, the Provincial Electricity Authority): 66 km/h max, 300 kg payload, ~45 THB per charge, 80–120 km range, chargeable from household power; so quiet you "don't have to shout" ([beartai, 21 Nov 2019](https://www.beartai.com/news/it-thai-news/380575)). In 2026 LOMO EV tuk-tuks with fixed 60–120 THB fares are reported (search snippet, [chiangmaiambassador](https://chiangmaiambassador.com/modern-transport-guide)) (unverified).
- **Rivalry with ride-hailing:** 2 Oct 2018, a tuk-tuk driver attacked a Grab driver at Arcade bus station; the tuk-tuk driver's licence was suspended and he was charged; he held only a private three-wheeler licence, no public-vehicle licence (up to 1 month prison and/or 1,000 THB fine); the Grab driver was fined 2,000 THB for using a private car commercially ([Thai Post](https://www.thaipost.net/main/detail/18928)).
- Driver income: a page claims a "2023 Chiang Mai Tourism Authority" survey of 520–680 THB/day net — no such body was found; treat as **unreliable** ([alibaba page](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/how-to-negotiate-fair-prices-with-tuk-tuk-drivers-in-chiang-mai-without-feeling-guilty.html)).

### Compared with Bangkok

- Thai regional styles: Bangkok "iconic, brightly colored"; Ayutthaya "rocket nose cone"; north-east motorbike-and-sidecar ([Tuk Tuk Club](https://www.thetuktukclub.com/2021/06/29/what-is-a-tuk-tuk/)). Tuk-tuks arrived as motorised samlors imported from Japan in the 1960s; the name imitates the engine's putter (same).
- Chiang Mai has far fewer tuk-tuks; red songthaews dominate; drivers "friendlier and far less aggressive" (search summaries of [backpackthailand](https://backpackthailand.com/guides/songthaew-tuk-tuk-guide) / [tuktuks.com](https://www.tuktuks.com/guide-to-tuk-tuks-in-thailand/); pages not fetched) (unverified).
- Standard tuk-tuks aren't built for mountains: The Tuk Tuk Club's mountain tuk-tuk "Flo" is a "Bangkok style Tuk Tuk, specially modified" for the bumps, "bigger, broader, with more padding", with a 650cc Daihatsu engine ([Tuk Tuk Club 2022](https://www.thetuktukclub.com/2022/11/10/thailands-first-female-mountain-tuk-tuk-driver/)). This suggests Doi Suthep could be an upgrade-gated route.

## 9. Scams, touts and what an honest driver does

- **Gem scam:** a "friendly local" near Tha Phae Gate or the moat says today is a tax-free gem-export day and offers a ride to a shop; stones are near-worthless and the tout takes commission. "Running since the 1990s", still active "particularly around Doi Suthep temple and the Old City" ([chiangmaiambassador, upd. Jun 2026](https://chiangmaiambassador.com/chiang-mai-scams)). Classic Bangkok form starts with "the temple is closed" and a "free" tuk-tuk ride ([wiki Gem scam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gem_scam)).
- **Commission tours:** very cheap or free temple tour whose route "always includes several shops" — fabric, tailors, jewellery ([chiangmaiambassador](https://chiangmaiambassador.com/chiang-mai-scams)); hotspots Tha Phae Gate, Wat Phra Singh, Night Bazaar ([thegoguy, Jan 2025](https://thegoguy.com/tourist-scams-in-chiang-mai-thailand-and-how-to-avoid-them/)).
- **"Closed temple" redirect**; fake monks collecting donations outside Wat Chedi Luang / Wat Phra Singh; fake goods at Warorot and the Night Bazaar (thegoguy).
- **Overcharging:** evening tuk-tuk mark-up of 200–300% at the Night Bazaar and Tha Phae Gate; Tha Phae Gate songthaews quoting 100–200 THB for a 30 THB shared ride, especially once Sunday Walking Street starts at 16:00 ([byklo, upd. 12 May 2026](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/)). Fair short city trip by tuk-tuk: 30–100 THB ([thegoguy, 24 Jan 2025](https://thegoguy.com/tourist-scams-in-chiang-mai-thailand-and-how-to-avoid-them/)).
- **Advice given to visitors** (so, what an honest driver looks like): agree the destination and price before boarding; no "quick stop"; drivers away from tourist ranks are safer; Grab removes ambiguity ([chiangmaiambassador](https://chiangmaiambassador.com/chiang-mai-scams)). Design synthesis (my own): honest = quotes first, fair price, direct route, no shop stops, helps with bags. That earns ratings and repeat passengers; the scam path gives short-term cash plus reputation and licence risk (cf. the 2018 licence suspension above).

## 10. Famous local things

- **Doi Suthep legend:** Sumanathera, a Sukhothai monk, brought a relic (Buddha's shoulder bone) to Lan Na. King Kue Na put one piece on a **white elephant** and released it; it climbed Doi Suthep (then "Doi Aoy Chang", Sugar Elephant Mountain), trumpeted three times and died there, so the temple was built on that spot. The other piece is at Wat Suan Dok. First stupa 1383; **309-step naga staircase**, the longest naga balustrade in Thailand; ~1,050–1,073 m; 15 km from the city; chedi 24 m; ~120,000 visitors/month ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wat_Phra_That_Doi_Suthep)).
- **Lanna architecture:** *kalae* houses, raised hardwood floors and wooden-tile roofs, with V-shaped carved *kalae* crossing at the gable tops ("kalae" from *ka-lang*, "crossing"); carvings are usually birds, also floral and cloud motifs ([wiki Kalae house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalae_house)). Teak construction and the "buffalo horns" reading (unverified; [changpuakmagazine](https://changpuakmagazine.com/en-article/KALAE/253408/) search summary only).
- **Yi Peng:** *yi* = two, *peng* = full-moon day: the full moon of the second Lanna month, usually November; mass release of *khom loi* sky lanterns; also *khom khwaen* hanging lanterns (traditionally blue, red, yellow, white, orange), *khom thue* carried on a stick, *khom fai* shaped paper lanterns and *khom pariwat* candle-driven revolving lanterns at temples ([wiki Yi Peng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Peng_festival)). Lanna *tung* flags as street decoration (unverified; not in the wiki article).
- **Songkran (April):** water fights; tuk-tuk passengers get soaked (see the 2009 Commons photo).
- **Bo Sang umbrellas:** ~10 km east of Chiang Mai, San Kamphaeng; bamboo-and-*saa* (mulberry) paper umbrellas, a craft that "originated in Burma"; festival "every year during the third weekend in January"; the 2022 edition (14–16 Jan) was the **39th** ([chiangmai-alacarte](https://www.chiangmai-alacarte.com/bo-sang-umbrella-village-chiang-mai/)). 2026 dates 16–18 Jan fit the third-weekend rule, but the "41st edition" claim conflicts with 2022 = 39th (annual since would make 2026 the 43rd) (unverified).
- **Silver of Wua Lai** (§2); **white elephants** (Chang Phueak monument, Doi Suthep legend).
- **Walking streets:** Sunday on Ratchadamnoen Rd, Tha Phae Gate → Wat Phra Singh, "roughly 4pm to 10–11pm" ([chiangmai-trip](https://www.chiangmai-trip.com/destinations/sunday-walking-street/)); Saturday on Wua Lai Rd, "smaller, calmer", hours (unverified).

### 11c. Loading-screen tips (17)

1. Chiang Mai means "new city". King Mangrai founded it in 1296.
2. "Tha Phae" means raft landing: river goods came ashore at the east gate.
3. Inner moat roads run anticlockwise and outer ones clockwise. Pick the right side of the moat.
4. A woman shouldn't sit beside a monk or hand him things directly. Give monks the back bench.
5. Red songthaews charge about 30 baht a head in town. Beat them on comfort, not price.
6. In Kham Mueang, "jao" makes a sentence polite and "lam" means delicious.
7. Hang a jasmine phuang malai on your mirror, an offering to Mae Yanang, goddess of journeys.
8. The Doi Suthep road was dug in 5 months 22 days and opened on 30 April 1935.
9. A white elephant carried the relic up Doi Suthep, trumpeted three times and died. The temple stands where it fell.
10. Wua Lai is the silversmiths' street; Wat Sri Suphan has a silver ordination hall.
11. Sunday Walking Street closes Ratchadamnoen Road from late afternoon. Plan your route around it.
12. Burning season (Feb–Apr) means fewer tourists. Mask up and chase the locals' trips.
13. Mahidol Road, Highway 1141, is your six-lane artery to the airport.
14. Tipping isn't expected on a tuk-tuk. Honest fares bring passengers back.
15. Yi Peng falls on the full moon of the second Lanna month, usually November. Expect lanterns and crowds.
16. The Superhighway is the last 10 km of Highway 11. It ends at Huay Kaew Road.
17. Chiang Mai tuk-tuk plates are yellow with green letters.

### 11d. Paint / decoration themes (12)

1. **Nakhon Blue (classic):** royal-blue body, yellow-gold pinstripe, red rims, black canopy, lit yellow TAXI box, chrome side bars. This is the most-photographed Chiang Mai look.
2. **Rot Daeng:** songthaew-red body with white lettering, a nod to the city's red trucks.
3. **Bo Sang Parasol:** cream *saa*-paper base with hand-painted flowers and a small parasol on the canopy.
4. **Khom Loi Night:** midnight-blue with warm lantern-glow LED strips. The party-tuk-tuk idea comes from Bangkok and Phuket.
5. **Kalae Teak:** teak wood-grain panels and V-shaped kalae horns crossed on the canopy front.
6. **Wua Lai Silver:** hammered repoussé silver panels, inspired by the silver ubosot.
7. **Chang Phueak (White Elephant):** white body with gold trim and elephant motifs.
8. **Songkran Splash:** clear rain curtains, waterproof seats and a water-pistol rack.
9. **Khao Soi Gold:** turmeric-curry yellow with a crispy-noodle pattern and a lime-green trim.
10. **Tung Lanna:** Lanna flags (*tung*) and hanging lanterns along the canopy edge.
11. **EV Green:** silent electric conversion in eco-green; perk: no shouting needed.
12. **Cooperative Taxi:** the yellow-blue split used by Chiang Mai's cooperative meter taxis.
