Compiled 2026-09-26 for *Tuk-Tuk Tycoon: Chiang Mai*, then independently fact-checked the same day against the cited pages, the EPPO xlsx, DLT registration statistics and Overpass. Every figure carries a source link and the year the figure refers to.
Tags:
- **(unverified)**: the page could not be opened, or the figure comes only from a search-engine snippet or an anecdote.
- **(derived)**: my own arithmetic on sourced inputs.
- **(old)**: the figure predates 2024; newer data was not found.

**How haggling works**
- The price is agreed before boarding. There is no meter and no charge for waiting time ([byklo](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/), [thaizer](https://www.thaizer.com/chiang-mai-transport/)).
- Tuk-tuks parked at the Night Bazaar or Tha Phae Gate quote high. A tuk-tuk flagged while moving on a main road costs less ([thaizer](https://www.thaizer.com/chiang-mai-transport/), [byklo](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/)).
- Where tuk-tuks cluster in 2026: Sunday Walking Street, Tha Phae Gate, Night Bazaar ([byklo](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/)).
- Ratios that come out of the sources above (derived):
  - Airport: opening quote ≈ 1.25–1.5 × settled.
  - Hotspots: opening quote ≈ 2–4 × fair ("200–300 % over fair price" read literally is 3–4 ×; 200–400 quoted vs 80–150 fair gives ~1.3–5 ×).
  - Songthaews parked at Tha Phae Gate after the Sunday Walking Street starts (16:00) quote tourists 100–200 THB for a 30 THB ride, i.e. 3–7 × ([byklo 2026](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/)).
  - Late night: quote ≈ 3–5 × the Grab price.
- **Tourist vs. local.** The tuk-tuk market is mainly foreign tourists who want the experience "occasionally" ([MGR 2017](https://mgronline.com/politics/detail/9600000042541)).
  - A Chiang Mai local said a driver dropped them mid-trip to pick up a foreigner ([Prachatai 2024](https://prachataienglish.com/node/11232)).
  - Locals mostly use songthaews and apps. No sourced "Thai price" figure was found (unverified).
- Common scams in Chiang Mai:
  - Overcharging, long detours, "temple closed", shop detours.
  - Detours go to souvenir, tailor and gem shops ([travelhiatus](https://travelhiatus.com/7-scams-in-chiang-mai-to-watch-out-for/), [thegoguy](https://thegoguy.com/tourist-scams-in-chiang-mai-thailand-and-how-to-avoid-them/)).

## 2. App-based options (Grab, Bolt, LOMO, others)

- **GrabTukTuk Electric**, launched in Chiang Mai on 4 Jun 2019 as a Southeast Asia first ([Grab press](https://www.grab.com/th/en/press/tech-product/%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%87%E0%B8%9A%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%9B%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%A7%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%87%E0%B8%9A%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%B8%E0%B9%8A%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%95/)).
  - Fare: **80 THB base + 12–15 THB/km, with no time charge** ([Blognone 2019](https://www.blognone.com/node/110133)). THE STANDARD also reports 12–15 THB/km ([2019](https://thestandard.co/grab-tuk-tuk/)).
  - Target was 450 EV tuk-tuks by the end of 2019.
  - Whether the category still runs in 2026: **unverified**.
- **Grab RodDaeng** books a red songthaew in the app ([chiangmaitraveller 2024](https://www.chiangmaitraveller.com/getting-around-chiang-mai/)).
- **GrabCar fares in Chiang Mai, 2025–26** ([byklo](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/)):
  - Airport → Old City: 80–180 THB.
  - Old City → Nimman: 60–90 THB.
  - Old City → Night Bazaar: 50–80 THB.
  - Old City → Doi Suthep: 250–400 THB one-way.
  - GrabBike is 30–40 % cheaper than GrabCar.
  - Surge at 07–09 and 16:30–19, rarely above 1.5×.
- **Other app fares, 2026**: Bolt 70–130 THB; Grab car 80–150 THB and bike 40–70 THB for typical city rides ([chiangmaiambassador](https://chiangmaiambassador.com/modern-transport-guide)).
- **LOMO EV tuk-tuk**, Chiang Mai, 2026:
  - App-booked, fixed price of **60–120 THB**.
  - Serves the Old City, Nimman, Santitham and the riverside ([chiangmaiambassador](https://chiangmaiambassador.com/modern-transport-guide)).
  - LOMO offers drivers "Fleet as a Service" and "Drive to Own", described as a path from renting to owning ([lomo2u.com](https://www.lomo2u.com/)). Fleet size and fares are not published.
- **Bolt** entered Chiang Mai in Dec 2020:
  - Fares 20 % below competitors, 0 % driver commission for the first 6 months ([Nation 2020](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/30399953)).
  - No Bolt tuk-tuk category was found (unverified).
- **Grab commission in Thailand**: 4-wheel drivers pay **25 %**; 2-wheel riders pay **15 %** ([APRN 2025 report](https://www.aprnet.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/APRN_EILER-Exploring-Grabs-Operations-in-Southeast-Asia.pdf), citing 2020 data). The tuk-tuk category rate is unverified.
- **Competition**: 3,000–4,000 ride-hailing vehicles operate in Chiang Mai. Legal operators complain that app cars are unregistered private vehicles ([TCIJ May 2024](https://www.tcijthai.com/news/2024/5/current/13641), [Prachatai Dec 2024](https://prachataienglish.com/node/11232)).

## 3. Red songthaew (rot daeng): the main competitor

- **Shared ride**: **30 THB per person** anywhere in the moat–Nimman–Night Bazaar triangle; 40–60 THB to Maya or the airport ([byklo 2026](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/)).
  - Airport 40 THB; leaves only when full ([catisoutoftheoffice 2025](https://catisoutoftheoffice.com/chiang-mai-songthaew/)).
  - "30–40 THB, moving toward 40 standard" ([chiangmaiambassador 2026](https://chiangmaiambassador.com/modern-transport-guide)).
- **Official cooperative caps** (Nakorn Lanna): 30 THB per person on circular routes; charter **max 250 THB per trip inside the Second Ring Road** ([Prachatai 2024](https://prachataienglish.com/node/11232)).
- **Charter or hire rates** ([byklo](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/)):
  - 150–300 THB/h.
  - Full-day temple loop 800–1,200 THB.
  - Private airport run 200 THB.
- **Doi Suthep songthaews** ([thainationalparks](https://www.thainationalparks.com/doi-suthep-pui-national-park/get-there), [catisoutoftheoffice](https://catisoutoftheoffice.com/chiang-mai-songthaew/)). They leave when about 10 passengers are aboard.
  - From the Chiang Mai Zoo / CMU queue, one-way per person: Doi Suthep 40 THB, Phuping Palace 70 THB, Doi Pui Hmong village 90 THB.
  - From Chang Phueak: 60 / 90 / 120 THB for the same stops.
  - A private round trip with waiting costs 200 THB (unverified snippet) to 300–500 THB (TripAdvisor snippet, unverified).
- **Fleet and driver income**:
  - Red songthaews fell from 2,800 (2017) to 2,465 (2018) to about 2,100 (May 2024) ([Prachatai](https://prachataienglish.com/node/11232)).
  - Driver income fell from about 1,500 THB/day to **500–600 THB/day** after Songkran 2024 ([THE STANDARD May 2024](https://thestandard.co/red-taxi-chiang-mai-income-drops/)).
  - Daily operating costs are 800–1,000 THB; about 150 THB fuel per trip on the new fixed routes ([Prachatai](https://prachataienglish.com/node/11232)).
- **Seasonality**: low season May–Oct, recovery Nov–Apr ([THE STANDARD 2024](https://thestandard.co/red-taxi-chiang-mai-income-drops/)).
- **Other public transport**: RTC city bus 20–30 THB. The airport R3 bus is 30 THB and takes 40 min ([byklo](https://www.byklo.rent/en/blog/how-to-get-around-chiang-mai/)).

**Engines.**
- Early tuk-tuks used 350 cc two-stroke engines. Today's use 650–660 cc 3-cylinder four-stroke engines, mostly running on LPG (some on NGV).
- The engines are second-hand units from Japan ([EVAT paper](https://www.engineeringforchange.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Tuk-Tuk-in-Thailand.pdf)).
- Many Bangkok tuk-tuks are 10–50 years old ([Thaiger Jul 2026](https://thethaiger.com/news/bangkok/thailand-considers-banning-petrol-tuk-tuks-in-central-bangkok)).
- Fuel mix of Chiang Mai's 1,039 registered for-hire tuk-tuks on 31 Aug 2026: 96 % LPG (996), 3 % electric (35) ([DLT registration statistics](https://web.dlt.go.th/statistics/)).

## 5. Running costs

### Fuel: LPG
- **EPPO price structure, Bangkok, 25 Sep 2026** ([xlsx](https://www.eppo.go.th/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/pt-price-st-2026-9-25.xlsx)):
  - LPG retail **25.87 THB/kg**, including an oil-fund subsidy of **−9.18 THB/kg**.
  - For comparison: Gasohol 95 E10 39.94, E20 34.94, Gasohol 91 39.57, diesel 41.44 THB/L.
- **Autogas per litre** ≈ 25.87 × 0.54 kg/L ≈ **14 THB/L** (derived; the density is my assumption).
  - EPPO's live station table only showed placeholder values, and its data endpoint returned 403.
  - Earlier pump prices: 13.65 THB/L in Jan 2023 ([Sanook, 4 Jan 2023](https://www.sanook.com/auto/84124/)); PTT 14.74–15.96 THB/L in Apr 2025 (search snippet, unverified).
  - One 2026 guide says "21–22 THB/L" ([checkraka](https://checkraka.app/guide/lpg-price-history-2026/)). This conflicts with EPPO's per-kg price (which still carries a −9.18 THB/kg oil-fund subsidy on 25 Sep 2026) and with the same guide's own 423 THB/15 kg cylinder; treat it as unreliable. The current autogas pump price per litre is **(unverified)**; a Kapook price board for 26 Sep 2026 lists PTT prices but no autogas LPG line.
- **NGV** (some tuk-tuks; 107 CNG for-hire tuk-tuks are registered in Bangkok, see §9): PTT **20.00 THB/kg** on 26 Sep 2026 ([Kapook price board](https://gasprice.kapook.com/gasprice.php)).
- **Cooking gas**: 15 kg cylinder ≈ 423 THB (≈28 THB/kg). Subsidies have been phased down since late 2025 ([checkraka, Apr 2026](https://checkraka.app/guide/lpg-price-history-2026/)).
  - Grab's 2019 release said tuk-tuks run on "LPG (cooking gas)" ([THE STANDARD 2019](https://thestandard.co/grab-tuk-tuk/)).
- **Consumption**. No direct measurement of an LPG tuk-tuk was found (unverified). Proxies:
  - Petrol tuk-tuk: **12–15 km/L** ([Biz Nex](https://www.biznexmotor.com/post/electric-tuktuk-vs-petrol-cost-comparison)).
  - LPG gives slightly fewer km/L than petrol; Sanook's example drops from 15 to 13 km/L ([Sanook](https://www.sanook.com/auto/84124/)).
  - This suggests roughly **9–13 km/L on LPG** (derived).
- **Monthly and daily fuel bills**:
  - LPG tuk-tuk fuel ≈ **6,000 THB/month** vs **1,400 THB/month** charging for an EV (Chiang Mai, 2019) ([Grab press](https://www.grab.com/th/en/press/tech-product/%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%87%E0%B8%9A%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%9B%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%94%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%A7%E0%B9%81%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%A3%E0%B9%87%E0%B8%9A%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%B8%E0%B9%8A%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%95/), [Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/in-focus/30370583)).
  - Bangkok daily fuel: 100 THB (Sep 2017, [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/blog/things-you-only-know-if-you-are-a-tuk-tuk-driver-092917)) to 200 THB (2014, old, [True Travel](https://truetravel.substack.com/p/the-impossible-economy-of-bangkoks)).
  - Petrol case: 225 THB/day at 80 km/day, assuming petrol at ~38 THB/L and 300 working days/year ([Biz Nex](https://www.biznexmotor.com/post/electric-tuktuk-vs-petrol-cost-comparison)).

### Electricity for EV charging
- **Consumption**: **0.08 kWh/km** ([Biz Nex](https://www.biznexmotor.com/post/electric-tuktuk-vs-petrol-cost-comparison)); **0.1 kWh/km** on a dynamometer test ([EVAT paper 2019](https://www.engineeringforchange.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Tuk-Tuk-in-Thailand.pdf)).
- **Range and charging**: 80–100 km, or 110–130 km; charging takes 3–8 h on AC or 30 min on DC.
- **Home tariff, 2026, excluding VAT**:
  - **3.88 THB/kWh** for Jan–Apr ([Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40059060)).
  - About 3.95 for May–Aug and 3.86 for Sep–Dec ([CapSolar, updated 8 Jul 2026](https://capsolar.co.th/en/knowledge/thailand-electricity-tariff); single source, Sep–Dec not cross-checked).
- **Other charging rates, early 2026** ([Green Energy Thailand](https://www.greenenergythailand.com/posts/much-charging-cost-thailand/)):
  - Home time-of-use off-peak ≈ 2.60 THB/kWh.
  - Public DC: PEA Volta 5.30–8.80; EA Anywhere (7-Eleven) 7.11; EleX/MEA 7.50; Onion 9.50 THB/kWh.
- **Daily charging cost**: ~29 THB at 80 km/day and 4.5 THB/kWh ([Biz Nex](https://www.biznexmotor.com/post/electric-tuktuk-vs-petrol-cost-comparison)).

### Maintenance and downtime
All figures from [Biz Nex](https://www.biznexmotor.com/post/electric-tuktuk-vs-petrol-cost-comparison) (undated):
- **Petrol/LPG tuk-tuk**: **12,000–25,000 THB/year**.
  - Oil every 3,000–5,000 km, plus filters, plugs, valves, clutch plates and exhaust.
  - Downtime of 8–20 days/year, valued at 1,000 THB/day lost.
  - A "major rebuild likely" by year 5.
- **EV**: 3,000–6,000 THB/year, under 5 days/year of downtime.
- **5-year operating cost**: about 490,000 THB for petrol vs 88,000 THB for EV.
- Tyres (4.00-12 size), engine overhaul and servicing prices were not found (unverified).

### Tax, insurance, inspection
- **Annual vehicle tax**, for-hire tuk-tuk of 500 kg: **185 THB/year**. It was cut 90 % to 18.50 THB for Oct 2022–Sep 2023 only ([Workpoint 2022](https://workpointtoday.com/annual-tax-reduction/)).
- **Compulsory insurance (Por Ror Bor)**, from a tariff in force since 1 Mar 2008 (old; whether it is still current is unverified) ([Mittare](https://mittare.data-insure.com/index.php/car-check/car-insure-prb)):
  - Three-wheeler outside Bangkok (Chiang Mai), private or for-hire: 400 premium + VAT + stamp = **430.14 THB/year**.
  - Bangkok: private 773.61; for-hire 1,547.22 THB/year.
- **Paperwork burden**: registered public vehicles in Chiang Mai pay tax twice a year and undergo inspection ("ต่อภาษีปีละ 2 ครั้ง ตรวจสภาพรถ"); app cars avoid this ([TCIJ May 2024](https://www.tcijthai.com/news/2024/5/current/13641); Prachatai's article does not state the twice-yearly detail). The inspection fee was not found (unverified).

## 6. Licensing, plates and quotas

- **Plate colours** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Thailand), citing the 2011 ministerial regulation):
  - For-hire tuk-tuk: yellow background, green characters (series ศข, สก, สข in Bangkok).
  - Private tricycle: white background, red characters.
  - A "TAXI" roof sign is required on new for-hire EV tuk-tuks: lit sign in the 2017 Chiang Mai rules ([MGR](https://mgronline.com/politics/detail/9600000042541)); white body with "TAXI" on the roof in the 2024 Bangkok EV quota ([TransTime](https://www.transtimenews.co/21277/)). Wikipedia does not state this.
  - White-plate (private) tuk-tuks touting tourists are the classic shop-detour operators ([nickjhowe 2011](https://www.nickjhowe.com/2011/12/beware-tuk-tuks-with-white-license-plates/)).
- **Chiang Mai quota** (Ministry of Transport notification signed 18 Apr 2017, in force 27 Apr 2017) ([MGR Online, 27 Apr 2017](https://mgronline.com/politics/detail/9600000042541)):
  - New for-hire tuk-tuk registrations are **electric only, capped at 450 vehicles**; the vehicle must be new and never registered before.
  - One person, one vehicle; the owner must be a Chiang Mai resident, must hold a **public three-wheeler driver's licence**, and must never have owned or possessed a for-hire tuk-tuk.
  - Owners must be a member of one of 3 bodies (see §9).
  - 10-year maximum service life from first registration; no transfer for 2 years except to heirs.
  - Vehicle spec: motor ≥4 kW, ≥45 km/h, max 1.5 × 4 × 2 m, lit "TAXI" roof sign.
  - **1,123 tuk-tuks** were registered in the province at the time.
  - The 450 EV plan was approved by DLT, with fares expected at 80–100 THB per trip ([CityLife 6 Jun 2017](https://www.chiangmaicitylife.com/citynews/local/450-electric-tuk-tuks-for-chiang-mai-approved-by-dlt/)).
  - Outcome by 31 Aug 2026: only **35** electric for-hire tuk-tuks are registered in Chiang Mai, against **996** LPG ones ([DLT registration statistics](https://web.dlt.go.th/statistics/), see §9). Existing LPG registrations evidently remain valid; the 450 EV slots are far from used up (derived).
- **Bangkok**:
  - New tuk-tuks cannot be registered. Owners buy old plates, which "have no expiry date" ([Bangkok Post 2019 via search](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/1674980/ev-group-urges-changes-to-3-wheeler-laws)).
  - A one-off EV tuk-tuk quota of **582** registrations was opened in Feb 2024, applications until 15 Mar 2024, lottery if oversubscribed. Applicants needed a public three-wheeler driver's licence, residence in Bangkok or a neighbouring province, and no tuk-tuk owned in the previous 180 days; motor ≥4 kW, ≥45 km/h ([TransTime 22 Feb 2024](https://www.transtimenews.co/21277/)).
  - 28 Jul–27 Aug 2026: DLT consulted on banning non-electric tuk-tuks from inner Bangkok ([Nation 31 Jul 2026](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40069238)).
- **Plate or licence value**:
  - Bangkok: **≈450,000 THB** (2014, old; [True Travel](https://truetravel.substack.com/p/the-impossible-economy-of-bangkoks)).
  - "Plates are expensive because tricycles can't be freely registered" ([Pantip, 23 Feb 2015](https://pantip.com/topic/33278870)).
  - **Chiang Mai plate value was not found (unverified).**
- **Driver's public three-wheeler licence** (ใบอนุญาตขับรถยนต์สามล้อสาธารณะ):
  - Valid 3 years; applicant ≥22 years old, holding a private three-wheeler licence for ≥1 year; medical check and criminal-record check.
  - Fee **150 THB** ([Grab TH blog Dec 2022](https://www.grab.com/th/en/blog/driver/%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%B4%E0%B8%98%E0%B8%B5%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B3%E0%B9%83%E0%B8%9A%E0%B8%82%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%9A%E0%B8%82%E0%B8%B5%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%98%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%93%E0%B8%B0/); same 150 THB / 3 years in [Tip Insurance May 2023](https://ft.tipinsure.com/NewsAndActivities/news_content_v4/Automobile/public-driving-license), which lists the public car licence at 300 THB).
  - Another guide lists 300 THB plus 5 THB application plus 100 THB card ≈ 405 THB, grouping tuk-tuks with cars ([Toyota Sure Feb 2026](https://www.toyotasure.com/contentdetail/contentdetailsui/id20260219-723)). The 300 THB figure is the public *car* licence fee; the DLT's own fee page is JS-rendered and could not be read (unverified).

## 7. Driver income and wages

- **Thai minimum wage**:
  - **Mueang Chiang Mai district 380 THB/day**, in force since **1 Jan 2025** (Wage Committee announcement No. 13); rest of the province 357 THB/day ([Chiang Mai News, 1 Jan 2025](https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/news/3543608/)).
  - Announcement No. 14, in force 1 Jul 2025, raised Bangkok and type 2–4 hotels and entertainment venues nationwide to 400 THB/day ([PRD](https://www.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/39/iid/402570)); the Chiang Mai rates stayed at 380/357 ([hrzoft](https://www.hrzoft.com/th/article/detail/minimum-wage-2569-by-province)).
  - No increase in 2026 as of 9 Jul 2026 ([hrzoft](https://www.hrzoft.com/th/article/detail/minimum-wage-2569-by-province)); Jul–Sep 2026 not checked (unverified).
- **Chiang Mai tuk-tuk income**:
  - Legal drivers "used to make up to 1,000 THB/day". By 2018 they made "a few hundred", "not enough to cover rents and fuel" ([Thaiger 8 Nov 2018](https://thethaiger.com/news/chiang-mai/grab-and-illegal-tuk-tuks-killing-chiang-mai-rental-company)).
  - Songthaew and tuk-tuk petition: income fell from ~2,500 to under 1,500 THB/day after Grab arrived ([Thaiger 17 May 2019](https://thethaiger.com/news/chiang-mai/chiang-mai-songthaews-and-tuk-tuk-drivers-complain-about-grab); probably gross).
- **Bangkok tuk-tuk income**:
  - Target gross **1,000 THB/day** gives ≈**500 THB take-home** after lease and fuel; the driver "only feels comfortable" at 2,500 THB/day ([True Travel, 2014, old](https://truetravel.substack.com/p/the-impossible-economy-of-bangkoks)).
  - "Good day" 500 THB; works 14 h/day, 7 days/week ([Blog the Globe 2018](https://blogtheglobe.com/17797-2/)).
- **How owners are paid**:
  - Dominant model: **the driver pays the owner a fixed daily or annual rent** (300–350/day), and the driver keeps fares and pays fuel and often repairs (sources in §4).
  - Commission/salary fleets appear only on the app side:
    - Grab keeps 25 % on 4-wheel rides.
    - MuvMi runs its own fleet (driver pay not found, unverified).
    - LOMO offers drive-to-own.

## Suggested game numbers (THB)

*These are suggestions derived from the sourced data above. They are not sourced facts. Change them freely for pacing.*

**Start**
- Starting cash: **3,000**. That is about one week of a Bangkok driver's ≈500/day take-home (2014 figure), or 8 days at the 380 Mueang Chiang Mai minimum wage.
- The player owns no vehicle. They rent a tired LPG tuk-tuk from "Uncle's garage" at **350/day**, paid at the end of each shift.
- They already hold a public three-wheeler licence.

**Fares**
- Street fare formula: **flag 40 + 18/km**, rounded to 10. This fits 100–120 for the ~4 km airport run and 80–150 for short hops.
- Customer types:
  - Locals: 0.8× and haggle hard. They are rare, because locals mostly ride songthaews.
  - Tourists: accept 1.0–1.5×.
  - The player may *quote* up to 3×. Acceptance falls off steeply above 1.5×, and repeat and rating penalties apply.
- Late night (after 22:00) near the Night Bazaar or Loi Kroh: a quote multiplier of 2–3× is tolerated.
- App job (unlocks mid-game): **80 + 13/km**, fixed. The platform keeps **20–25 %**. No haggling, but there is steady volume.

**Trip catalogue** (fare = what the player collects)

| Trip | Fare |
|---|---|
| Old City hop | 60–150 |
| Old City ↔ Nimman | 100–150 |
| Old City ↔ Night Bazaar | 60–150 by day; 200–400 quoted late |
| Airport → Old City | 100–150 (opening quote 150) |
| Arcade bus terminal → Old City | 120–200 |
| Train station → Tha Phae | 70–150 |
| Hourly charter | 250/h |
| Half-day temple loop | 600 |
| Full day | 1,000–1,500 |
| Guided temple tour (needs a "guide" upgrade) | 1,000/group |
| Doi Suthep | **locked for LPG tuk-tuks** ("can't climb"). Unlocks with an EV or an engine upgrade at 400 return. Otherwise the fare is lost to songthaews (40–60 per person) |

**Competitors setting price ceilings**
- Songthaew: 30/person, charter cap 250.
- Grab car: 60–90 for Old City ↔ Nimman.
- LOMO-style EV app: 60–120.

**Running costs**
- Fuel:
  - LPG ≈ **1.4/km** (14/L ÷ ~10 km/L). A 100 km day ≈ 140, matching the sourced 100–225/day.
  - EV at home ≈ **0.35/km** (0.09 kWh × 3.9). EV at a public DC charger ≈ **0.65/km**.
- Upkeep per day while in service:
  - LPG maintenance **55** (≈16,500/year ÷ 300 days).
  - EV maintenance **15**.
  - Insurance + tax ≈ **2** (≈430 Por Ror Bor outside Bangkok + 185 tax ≈ 615 per year).
  - A "twice-yearly inspection/renewal" event every 180 days: **500** (fee unverified).
- Breakdowns:
  - LPG: **4 %/day** (≈12 days/year). Each costs one lost day and a 500–3,000 repair.
  - EV: **1 %/day**.
  - Engine rebuild at about year 5: **25,000**.

**Vehicles**

| Vehicle | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Rusty used LPG, yellow plate | **150,000** | 4–6 % breakdown rate |
| Good used LPG, yellow plate | **200,000** | |
| Used EV with plate | **210,000** | |
| New EV | **290,000** | Thai King list price with battery and registration (§4); a 7-seat NEX2-class EV could sit at ~320,000 (price unverified) |
| EV conversion kit for an owned LPG tuk-tuk | **200,000** | cheaper than the 2019 prototype at ~340,000, for pacing |
| Unplated private tuk-tuk | **30,000** | can't legally take fares; fines or confiscation risk; shop-detour driver trope |
| Resale | 60 % of purchase, falling 5 %/year | |

**Licences and quota**
- Chiang Mai EV quota of **450** plates total; one per person. The player's company must use members or drivers to register vehicles, which makes a plate slot a hiring gate. Only 35 were in use by Aug 2026, so EV slots are plentiful early on.
- LPG plates can only be bought second-hand from retiring owners, at a **plate premium of 100,000–150,000** (Chiang Mai value unverified; Bangkok ≈450,000 in 2014). The city has ~1,040 registered for-hire tuk-tuks but only ~100 working ones, so the rival pool is ~100 active drivers.
- Driver's public licence **150** (3-year renewal).
- Annual vehicle tax **185**.

**Hiring and fleet**
- Model A, "rent-out", the default: a hired driver pays the player **350/day** per tuk-tuk and keeps the fares. The player covers maintenance, insurance and tax; the driver pays fuel.
- Model B, "salaried": the player pays **400/day** (above the 380 minimum wage) plus **10–20 %** of fares. The player keeps the fares and pays fuel.
- Hiring fee **1,000**. Morale drops if net driver pay falls below ~500/day.

**Side income**
- Shop-detour kickback: **100/shop** or a **150 fuel voucher**. Each detour risks a rating or reputation hit and a tourist complaint.
- Ad panel on a tuk-tuk: 1,000–3,000/month per vehicle (unverified).

**Season modifiers**
- High season Nov–Apr: demand 1.3×.
- Low season May–Oct: 0.7×.
- Songkran, Loy Krathong, Yi Peng: event spikes (see calendar.md).
